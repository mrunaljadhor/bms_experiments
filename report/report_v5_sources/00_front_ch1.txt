=== ABSTRACT
Standard range estimators till date have typically scaled the state of charge (SOC) by a fixed consumption figure, assuming that the vehicle is driving in a perfect scenario, with no hilly roads and zero traffic. Traffic and terrain parameters are ignored by these estimators. To address this shortcoming, the proposed framework iBMS integrates data-driven battery-state tracking with external route physics and congestion for lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) two-wheelers.
The software stack comprises a long short-term memory (LSTM) network to portray complex cell dynamics directly from measured time-series vectors: terminal voltage, current and temperature. Simultaneously, the route-energy model combines elevation metrics, real-time traffic telematics and a road-load velocity parameter into mode-oriented distance-to-empty (DTE) values for ECO and SPORT profiles. Control decision is made via an Adaptive Mode Switching Algorithm (AMSA) that extends feasibility checking before a trip to actively enforce drive-mode overrides or mandate charging stops across safe, critical or infeasible predictions. A Next.js dashboard and a Node.js backend, both deployed in the cloud, integrate these functions.
On applying this on 8 route directions throughout India, a terrain-unaware range estimator overestimated the ECO range uphill by 53–60 % and endorsed a 60 km mountain route that AMSA discards even from a full charge. No initial SOC is required by the LSTM model. It achieves a mean absolute error of 0.20 % on held-out synthetic discharges and 3.17 % on unseen drive cycles of a public LiFePO4 dataset, where an extended Kalman filter with a measured open-circuit-voltage curve achieved 0.96 %. The int8 model occupies 67.6 KiB and runs in 0.41 ms on a laptop CPU, and SHAP shows a reliance on the recent terminal voltage for explainability. AMSA decides on a lower SOC bound, which cuts unsafe route acceptance from 0.71 % to 0.007 % at the cost of more refusals, going from 1.6 % to 8.2 %.
Keywords: adaptive drive-mode switching, distance-to-empty, electric two-wheelers, explainable artificial intelligence, LiFePO4 batteries, long short-term memory, state-of-charge estimation, uncertainty quantification.

=== CHAPTER 1 INTRODUCTION
## 1.1 Background & Motivation
Even though electric two-wheelers offer low-cost urban mobility, their packs hold little energy reserve. The electric two-wheeler studied in this project carries a LiFePO4 pack of 63.5 V and an estimated 30.9 Ah, about 1.96 kWh. An error of a few hundred watt-hours in the planned trip energy of a pack of about 2 kWh decides whether the rider will arrive or is stuck somewhere.
Most of the range estimators calculate the distance-to-empty (DTE) by scaling the state of charge (SOC) with a fixed consumption value, as if every kilometre, and every two different routes with the same distance, will cost the same energy. References [1]–[3] state that route energy depends on topography, traffic and speed. Uphill routes require energy well above the flat-road values [4], [5], and downhills return part of it through regenerative braking [6], that is, motion is turned back into battery energy which is then stored. A flat-road DTE is hence optimistic on climbs, which is exactly where accuracy matters most.
Research regarding battery management systems (BMS) has been oriented so far to data-driven estimation of the SOC, the state of health (SOH) and the remaining useful life (RUL). LSTM networks model the time-series behaviour of cells [7], [8], explainability techniques make such models inspectable [9], [10], and edge and federated learning keep telemetry on the vehicle [11]–[13], ensuring privacy. These battery estimators do not see the trip in advance, and energy-aware routing barely uses the measured battery state. The Automated Drive Mode Switching and Recommendation Algorithm (AMSA) [14] connects the two by changing to ECO mode when the remaining distance of the trip exceeds the range left in the current mode. The range check of AMSA uses instantaneous consumption (power / speed) and cannot predict the cumulative climbs and congestion of a particular route.
The proposed software stack, iBMS, solves this problem for LiFePO4 two-wheelers. Before the trip begins, the route is returned with live traffic and its elevation profile; the cumulative grades, congestion and assumed cruising speed are then converted into ECO and SPORT DTE values scaled by the battery state. AMSA is extended from real-time mode selection to a route-level check. A leg is basically one section of a trip between two stops. A leg possible in SPORT mode stays under manual control, a leg possible only in ECO mode triggers an enforced ECO-Lock, and an impossible leg requires a charging stop.
## 1.2 Problem Statement
Conventional range estimation in electric two-wheelers ignores the terrain and traffic of the planned route and the health of the battery, so it can approve trips that strand the rider, especially on hill routes. Real-time mode switching reacts only once a climb has begun, and data-driven SOC estimators are rarely benchmarked fairly or connected to trip decisions. The problem addressed is to design and evaluate a framework that estimates the SOC of a LiFePO4 pack without an initial SOC, computes the energy of a planned route from its terrain, traffic and speed, checks the route leg by leg before departure using an uncertainty-aware SOC, and enforces ECO mode or a charging stop when needed.
## 1.3 Objectives of the Project
- Objective 1: a route-energy model that brings cumulative grades from a 30 m elevation model, a real-time traffic-jam ratio, a road-load speed factor and SOH-scaled pack energy into mode-specific DTE.
- Objective 2: a route-level AMSA that enforces ECO-Lock or a charging stop before the start of the trip, evaluated on eight real route directions against a terrain-unaware baseline estimator.
- Objective 3: a leak-free LSTM SOC estimator for a 63.5 V LiFePO4 pack that needs no initial SOC, compared with Coulomb counting, an extended Kalman filter (EKF) and a constant predictor.
- Objective 4: the estimator quantized to a 67.6 KiB int8 model, explained with SHAP and given a calibrated lower SOC bound for risk-aware decisions, as it makes unsafe trip acceptances much rarer.
- Objective 5: a web dashboard and REST backend, deployed in the cloud, that integrate these functions, and an IEEE conference paper describing the work.
The mid-term objective of federated learning was evaluated and replaced by uncertainty quantification: federated averaging reached 8.99 % MAE against 3.17 % for centralized training on CALCE, and its model updates needed more data transfer than the raw data (Appendix C).
## 1.4 Scope & Limitations
The project covers a software framework (data processing, estimation models, route-energy model, AMSA, dashboard and backend) with offline experiments, and a single-cell BMS bench prototype. ECO-Lock enforcement is at present a software command at the rider's interface. The field data come from stationary tests, so the route results are model-based evaluations on real routes with live traffic and measured terrain, not on-road validation. The main limitations are listed in Section 6.4.
